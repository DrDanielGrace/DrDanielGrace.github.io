Week 1 — Foundation
DAY 01 Setup + Algebra Foundations + First Archviz Scene
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Resume Algebra 1 from where you stopped. Today's focus is variables and expressions. Write out every example by hand, not just watch. The habit of writing matters. Ask yourself: "what is this expression actually describing in the real world?" That question will matter later.
Afternoon — Unreal Archviz Setup 3-3.5 hrs
Install Twinmotion from Epic Games Launcher. Also open Fab.com in browser and download 2-3 free interior asset packs (search "interior architecture free"). Watch William Faucher's "Unreal Engine 5 Archviz for Beginners" intro video on YouTube. Then open Unreal Engine 5, create a new blank project, and just place one room with walls, a floor, and one light source. Nothing fancy. Just get a scene rendering today.
Bridge Note
Variables in algebra (x, y) are the same thing as parameters in Unreal Engine. When you set a room width to "5 meters" that 5 is a variable. Start seeing math and engine work as the same language.
DAY 02 Equations + Lumen Lighting Basics
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Linear equations, one variable. Solve 10 problems by hand. Focus on what "solving for x" actually means physically. If x is the unknown wall length, what equation describes it?
Afternoon — Lumen Lighting 3 hrs
Watch William Faucher "Lumen Lighting in UE5" tutorial. Then in your scene from Day 1, enable Lumen in Project Settings. Add a Directional Light (sun), a Sky Light, and one interior Rect Light to simulate a window. Adjust intensity values. Notice how changing the number changes the feel. Screenshot your best result.
Bridge Note
Light intensity in Unreal is measured in Lux or Candela, which are real physics units. Sunlight is around 100,000 Lux. A living room lamp is around 300 Lux. Your equation today: if you want a room to feel like 3pm sunlight, what intensity value do you input? That is applied math.
DAY 03 Graphing + Materials and Surfaces
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Graphing linear equations. Plot 5 lines on paper. Understand slope (rise over run). What does a steep slope look like vs a gentle one? You will use this when animating camera movements in Unreal.
Afternoon — Quixel Materials 3 hrs
Open Quixel Bridge inside Unreal Engine (it is built in, top menu). Download 5 free surface materials: wood floor, white wall paint, concrete, glass, and a fabric or curtain material. Apply them to surfaces in your room. Focus on the Roughness and Metallic sliders in the material editor. Roughness = 0 is a mirror. Roughness = 1 is completely matte. Experiment with every surface until the room looks like a real interior.
Bridge Note
Roughness and Metallic values in Unreal go from 0.0 to 1.0. That is a number line. Graphing where different materials sit on that line (glass at 0.0 roughness, concrete at 0.8 roughness) is literally plotting points on a graph.
DAY 04 Systems of Equations + Camera and Composition
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Systems of two equations. Two unknowns, two equations, one solution. Think of it as finding the exact point where two lines cross. In 3D space, every object has an X and Y coordinate. A system of equations finds where two objects or paths meet.
Afternoon — Cinecamera Setup 3 hrs
Add a CineCamera Actor to your scene. Set Focal Length to 24mm for wide interior shots. Set Aperture (f-stop) to f/2.8 for a shallow depth of field effect. Place the camera at human eye level (around 160cm height). Frame a corner of the room using the rule of thirds. Watch one short video on "architectural photography composition" on YouTube to understand what clients expect to see. Take 3 different camera angles of your room and compare them.
Bridge Note
Camera position in Unreal is X, Y, Z coordinates. Moving a camera from one point to another is literally solving for where two coordinate values meet. Your systems of equations practice is giving you the mental model for 3D spatial reasoning.
DAY 05 Inequalities + Furniture and Asset Placement
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Inequalities and their graphs. Greater than, less than. This is about ranges, not exact values. A sofa must be less than 3 meters wide to fit in a room. The room temperature must stay between 18 and 26 degrees. Inequalities describe real constraints.
Afternoon — Full Room Build 3.5 hrs
Today you build your first complete room. Go to Fab.com or Sketchfab and download a free furniture pack. Place a sofa, a coffee table, a TV unit, a floor lamp, and some decorative items. Think about spacing: furniture should not touch walls, allow walking paths of at least 90cm. Add curtains or blinds to your window opening. Add a rug under the sofa. By end of day your scene should look like a real living room someone could move into.
Bridge Note
Furniture spacing constraints are inequalities. "The gap between sofa and TV must be more than 2 meters for comfortable viewing" is literally: gap > 2. You are solving interior design with algebra.
DAY 06 Review Week + First Render Output
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math Review 1 hr
Khan Academy: Do the Algebra 1 unit test or practice quiz for everything covered so far. Write down any topic you got wrong and note it for next week. This is not punishment, it is data about where your edge is.
Afternoon — High Quality Render 3.5 hrs

When done, open the image. This is your first portfolio piece. Save it somewhere safe.
Week 1 Milestone: You have a complete furnished room rendered at 4K with proper lighting, materials, and camera composition. That is a real portfolio piece. The math you did this week (variables, equations, graphs, inequalities) all showed up in real decisions you made in Unreal.

Week 2 — Intermediate Skills + Portfolio Ready
DAY 08 Functions + Exterior Scene Begins
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Introduction to functions. f(x) means: give me an input, I will give you an output. A render pipeline is a function. Give it a scene, it outputs an image. Your Python trading bot will be a function. Master this concept. Do 15 practice problems.
Afternoon — Start an Exterior Scene 3.5 hrs
Start a new project. This time build the outside of a building. Use Unreal's landscape tools to create a simple flat ground. Add a building facade using basic geometry or a downloaded free model from Fab. Add trees and plants from Quixel Bridge (search "grass", "tree", "shrub"). Set up a sky with UE5's Sky Atmosphere and Volumetric Clouds. Get a realistic golden hour sun by rotating your Directional Light to a low angle. Exterior scenes are what Nigerian and international real estate developers actually want to show buyers first.
Bridge Note
The Sun's position in your scene is controlled by angles. Rotating the Directional Light by degrees is trigonometry. You are not there yet in math but you are already using it intuitively.
DAY 09 Function Graphs + Walkthrough Animation
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Graphing functions. Understand how input values produce a curve, not just a line. Animation curves in Unreal Engine work exactly this way: the shape of the curve determines how fast or slow something moves. An "ease in" curve is a specific mathematical function shape.
Afternoon — Sequencer Walkthrough 3.5 hrs
Open Unreal Sequencer (Cinematics menu). Create a Level Sequence. Add your CineCamera to the sequence. Set a keyframe at the start position and another keyframe 5 seconds later at a different position. Press play. The camera moves between them. Now right-click the keyframe curve and change it from Linear to Ease In/Ease Out. See how the motion feels more cinematic. Spend the rest of the session building a 15-30 second walkthrough of your exterior scene. This is a deliverable you can show clients.
DAY 10 Ratios and Proportions + Scale and Accuracy
Math Archviz
▼
Morning — Math 1 hr
Khan Academy: Ratios, rates, and proportions. If a real building is 10 meters tall and your Unreal scene is in centimeters, that building must be 1000 units tall. Getting scale right is pure proportional math. A room that looks "off" is usually wrong scale.
Afternoon — Accurate Scale + Python Mini Script 3.5 hrs
First, go through your Week 1 room scene and check scale. Is the door 210cm tall? Is the sofa 85cm high? Is the ceiling 270cm? Fix anything that looks wrong using the Transform panel. Then, write a tiny Python script (10-15 lines) that takes a real-world measurement in meters and converts it to Unreal units (centimeters). Run it in VS Code. This is your first bridge between coding, math, and Unreal. Save the script to your GitHub.
Bridge Note
This Python script is literally a function. Input: meters. Output: centimeters. f(x) = x * 100. You wrote math as code. This is exactly what you will do in aerospace simulation and in trading bots. Same skill, different domain.

Math Archviz

Spend the last hour researching what archviz freelancers charge on Fiverr and Upwork. Note the range. Write down three price points you could offer: a basic package, a standard package, and a premium package.
